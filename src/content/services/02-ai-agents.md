AI agents can now read documents, draft responses, move data between systems, and complete multi-step work while a person reviews the result.  The hard part is choosing the right work to hand them and doing it safely.

I run multi-agent AI systems myself, on infrastructure I built and operate.  That practice shapes my advice.  I know where agents save real time and where they still need a person close by.

## How an engagement works

- **Opportunity assessment.**  I talk with the people doing the work and map where their time goes.  You receive a short list of candidate workflows ranked by value, risk, and effort.
- **Pilot.**  We choose one workflow, build or configure the agent, and measure it against the current process.
- **Scale with governance.**  Pilots that prove their value move into production with access controls, logging, human review points, and a clear owner.

## Where agents tend to help first

- Intake and triage of email, forms, and requests.
- Drafting routine documents from templates and prior work.
- Research and summarization across internal files.
- Moving data between systems that do not connect to each other.

## Build or buy

Many needs can be met with tools you already license, such as Microsoft Copilot or Claude.  When a workflow needs something custom, I design it and my delivery partner, [Number Zoo](https://numberzoo.ai), builds it.  I tell you up front which path I recommend and why.
