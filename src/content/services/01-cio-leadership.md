Many organizations of 25 to 300 people make CIO-level decisions without a CIO.  Security, vendor contracts, cloud spending, and now AI adoption fall to an operations lead, a managed service provider, or whoever knows the most about computers.  A fractional CIO gives you executive technology judgment for a set number of days each month, without the cost of a full-time executive.

An interim engagement covers a gap.  If your technology leader has left, or a major project needs a steady owner, I step in, stabilize the work, and help you hire or develop the right successor.

## What I take on

- A technology strategy and roadmap tied to your business plan, with a cost, an owner, and a reason for each project.
- The technology budget, including licensing, renewals, and cloud spending.
- A security program built on NIST principles, with immutable backups and a disaster recovery plan.
- Oversight of vendors and managed service providers, so the people you pay are accountable for results.
- Plain-language reporting to your leadership team and board.
- A practical plan for AI that fits the rest of the roadmap.

## What I bring to it

I spent 12 years as CIO of a professional services firm of about 410 people, leading a 26-person team and a budget of more than $7M.  I moved that firm to the cloud in 2018, gave every employee full remote capability in 2019, and moved its accounting platform out of the server room without missing a close.  Those projects taught me that good technology leadership is mostly sequencing, communication, and follow-through.

## How it starts

We begin with a conversation about where things stand.  If it makes sense to continue, I start with a short assessment of your systems, spending, and risks, and we agree on the number of days per month that fits.  The goal is a stronger organization, including the people who will run it after I step back.
